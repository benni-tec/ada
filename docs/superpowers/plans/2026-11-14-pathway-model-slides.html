Junction 2026 · Equal Care Across Finland

Finland loses cancer patients to time. The Nordic standards show where.

A patient pathway model: whether a presentation would start a fast-track pathway in Norway or Sweden, when each step is due, how many days Finland's pathways run behind, and what that costs. Built on ADA with every figure traced to its source.

Educational use, synthetic profiles only. The platform is not for patient data.

3 · What Norway and Sweden have

A trigger and a clock. Finland has neither.

Trigger"Well-founded suspicion": an imaging finding, a metastasis, repeated haemoptysis.
7days to the first specialist visit
28days to the end of work-up
42–44days to the start of treatment

Sweden also names the symptoms that send a patient to imaging within six days. Finland has no national trigger, no standard times and no cancer-specific waiting-time statistics.

Helsedirektoratet, Pakkeforløp for lungekreft (3 July 2026). Regionala cancercentrum, SVF lungcancer v1.4 (2024). Colorectal and pancreas standards read the same way.

4 · Does it matter?

After Denmark's pathways: lung survival 11 → 20 %

Three-year relative survival among symptomatic primary-care patients, before and after the pathways, adjusted for stage, comorbidity, education and income: excess hazard ratio 1.30 (1.09–1.55) before vs after.

The authors name the caveat themselves: earlier diagnosis makes survival look longer even when death comes no later. Lead time is on every slide that follows.

Jensen, Tørring & Vedsted, BMC Cancer 2017.

5 · The tool at the point of care

A woman of 64, lung cancer, day 71

  • Cough for eight weeks, smoker, at a health centre: suspicion met, Sweden
  • X-ray on day 27 shows a lesion: pathway would start, Norway and Sweden
  • First specialist visit met, work-up 3 days late, treatment start overdue by 1 day. No plan booked.
  • 20 days behind target so far, most of them before the X-ray.

This part needs no hazard ratio. The urgency comes from a standard that exists next door.

7 · How it is built

One parameter per arrow, so nothing counts twice

  • Baseline: the Nordic level by site, sex and age group, from NORDCAN through ADA, cited by operator and run.
  • Days from the trigger to treatment beyond target: Hanna (meta-analysis), Heiden (one cohort).
  • Days before the trigger: Sud's per-day rate, a toggle, off by default.
  • Education: Zai's direct effect, carried but never shown as a lower figure.
  • The Finland residual: what the days do not explain, as one factor, shown not hidden.

Westreich & Greenland, Am J Epidemiol 2013, on why a product of adjusted hazard ratios double-counts.

10 · What it is not

Not a prognosis. Not a device. Not validated on Finnish patients.

  • Lead time and the waiting-time paradox are stated on every result.
  • Nordic baseline, Finnish education gradient, American and English delay effects.
  • Educational use with synthetic profiles; a banner on every page. A clinical version would be a medical device (MDR Rule 11) and need Findata.
